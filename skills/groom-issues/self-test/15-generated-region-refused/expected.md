# Expected outcome — fixture 15 (right content, wrong owner)

The fix is mechanical by every test the skill applies. It is inside a generated block.

**Expected decision:** `REFUSE-GENERATED`, with the reason:

```
#29 — missing cross-reference is inside the nwave:status block. Refused.
      That region is regenerated by phil:nwave-issue-board, so this edit would be
      destroyed on the next refresh and would disagree with its source until then.
      Fix it at the generator, or outside the markers.
```

**This is fixture `04` from the other side.** Slice 01 refuses to *report* a missing generated line as
a body defect; slice 02 refuses to *write* into the region that holds it. The principle is the same one
and it is about ownership, not content: a human owns their prose, a generator owns what is between its
markers. A correct string in the wrong owner's territory is not a fix — it is a change with a scheduled
deletion.

**Gate failures:**

- Writing it because the content is right and the classification says mechanical. Mechanical is about
  the derivability of the content; this refusal is about who owns the bytes. Both must pass.
- Editing just outside the markers to place the reference "nearly" where it belongs. That leaves the
  generated region still wrong, adds a second statement that will drift from it, and hides the problem
  from the next reader instead of reporting it.
- Refusing silently, or reporting it as skipped without the reason. The reason is what tells the user
  where the fix actually belongs — and pointing at the generator is the only part of this outcome that
  leads anywhere.
- Refusing the whole issue when only part of it is generated. Defects outside the markers on #29 are
  ordinary and are handled ordinarily.
