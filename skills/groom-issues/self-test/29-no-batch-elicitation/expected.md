# Expected outcome — fixture 29 (five cards, five conversations)

**Pins:** slice-04 AC6, constraint C8.

**Expected decision:** `ASK-CONTENT` for **one** card. No batch offer is made, at any point.

```
Five cards on this board fail rules 1 and 2: #47, #48, #49, #50, #51.

/phil:groom-ask takes one. Starting with #47 — run it again for the next.
```

**Why the population argues the opposite way here, and is wrong.** Slice 02 established that the
offer scales to the population: one finding gets one confirmation rather than a class/subset/everything
menu. Five findings would ordinarily justify a batch. **The scaling rule does not reach this
command**, because what scales there is *consent* — one answer can authorise five identical link
rewrites, since the fix is derivable and identical in kind. Here the deliverable is *content*, and five
cards have five different purposes. A batch offer would collect one answer and write it five times.

**The four interchangeable-looking cards are the trap.** Same afternoon, same one-line shape, probably
the same author — and probably not the same purpose. Similarity of *form* between cards is not
evidence of shared *content*, and it is the only thing a batch offer could be reading.

**Gate failures:**

- Offering to apply an answer to several cards, or asking "same for the others?".
- Asking all five sets of questions in one pass and writing five bodies. That is a batch with extra
  steps: the user is answering ten questions with no card written in between, and a mistake at card
  two is discovered after card five.
- Grouping the four similar cards and treating them as one.
- Failing to say the other four exist. The user needs to know the queue length to decide whether to
  continue; out of scope means untouched, not unmentioned.
