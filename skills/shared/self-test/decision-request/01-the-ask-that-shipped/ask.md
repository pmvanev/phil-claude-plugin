Everything here is about the moment a tool stops and asks you to choose. First: how much discovery to do
before writing anything down — the cheap version maps the one path where you're asked something and
answer it; the fuller version also maps what happens when the question is malformed, which is the actual
complaint. Second: who this is for. Every past feature here got its own named user, but this one's user is
whoever happens to be at the keyboard — the person who was doing something else and got interrupted by a
question. That may be a new person to describe, or it may just be an existing one wearing a different hat.
Third: the 200 words. If it's a hard ceiling, a test can measure it and fail; if it's roughly-that-length,
no test can, and the standard becomes advice.
