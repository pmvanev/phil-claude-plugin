**Why stopping is the answer and not a smaller ranking.** The tempting partial move is to rank the three
feature cards and leave the eight alone. That writes a real order over an incomplete population — and the
eight will become rows inside two of the three, so two of the ranked cards are about to change size while
their positions stay. The order would be correct on the day it was written and wrong the moment the
consolidation lands, which is this suite's recurring failure shape.

**The majority is the trap.** Eight of eleven cards sort into an obvious sequence, and that sequence is the
most confident-looking thing on the board.

**Gate failures:**

- Writing any position. Including over the three feature cards alone.
- Ranking the slice cards because they are the majority, or because their order is obvious.
- **Adjusting an existing order rather than re-deriving** once the board is consolidated. The old order
  ranked slices; nudging it preserves a sequence whose subject changed. This is the fifth entry in *What
  will go wrong*.
- Treating the sub-issue edges as a goal hierarchy. A goal is a milestone; a parent is a container.
